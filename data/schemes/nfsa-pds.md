# National Food Security Act and the ration card

## What this scheme gives you

Subsidised, and at present free, foodgrain through the public distribution
system. Priority households are entitled to 5 kg of grain per person per
month, and Antyodaya Anna Yojana households get 35 kg per household per
month. The grain is rice, wheat or coarse grain, depending on the state.

Coverage is capped by law at 75 per cent of the rural population and 50 per
cent of the urban population, so eligibility depends on where the household
sits in the state's list rather than on a single income cut-off. The free
distribution currently in force is a time-limited extension, so check the
current end date before assuming grain will stay free.

## Who is eligible

- Priority households identified by the state under NFSA, and Antyodaya Anna
  Yojana households, which are the poorest and get the larger 35 kg quota.
- Households are identified using SECC data and state-level criteria, and the
  final list is published by the state.
- A ration card is issued per family, and the family can draw grain from any
  fair price shop after the card is linked to Aadhaar and the shop's
  electronic point of sale machine.

## Who is NOT eligible

Households that are not in the state's priority or AAY list, and households
that are already covered by another state food scheme in place of NFSA.
Households that move interstate must transfer the card rather than apply for a
second one, because one family can hold only one ration card.

## Documents you need

Aadhaar of all family members, proof of address, and the existing ration card
if the household already has one. A photograph of the head of the family is
taken at the fair price shop or the circle office.

## How to apply

Apply at the food and civil supplies office or the nearest fair price shop,
or through the state's online ration card portal. After verification, the card
is printed with the family's details. Grievances about a dealer refusing grain
or charging money go to the state food department or the national helpline
1967, and the entitled quantity is printed on the card so a shortfall is easy
to prove.

## What to watch out for

Keep the printed receipt from the electronic point of sale machine: it shows
the quantity and the price, and it is the evidence needed for a complaint.
Deletion from the list must follow a notice, so a card cancelled without
notice can be challenged before the district grievance redressal officer.
