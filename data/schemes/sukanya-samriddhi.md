# Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana

## What this scheme gives you

A long-term tax-free small savings account for a girl child, opened in her
name at a post office or an authorised bank. The account earns a fixed rate of
interest set by the government every quarter, which has been around 8 per cent
in recent years. Interest and the maturity amount are exempt from income tax
under the EEE treatment, so there is no tax on the contribution, the interest
or the withdrawal.

The account runs for 21 years from the date of opening. Deposits have to be
made for the first 15 years, after which the account keeps earning interest
until maturity.

## Who is eligible

- A girl child below 10 years of age, through a parent or legal guardian.
- A maximum of two accounts per family, one for each daughter. A third
  account is allowed only in the case of twins or triplets born in a second
  order of birth.
- The girl must be a resident of India.

## Who is NOT eligible

A girl who has turned 10 years old cannot open a new account, and a third
daughter in a family is outside the limit unless she is a twin or triplet.
Non-resident Indians are restricted by the residency rules, so the exact
position should be checked with the bank before opening.

## Documents you need

The girl's birth certificate, her Aadhaar, the parent's or guardian's Aadhaar
and address proof, and a photograph. If the account is opened by a guardian,
proof of guardianship is needed.

## How to apply

Open the account at any post office or authorised bank branch, or through the
India Post or bank online channel. The minimum deposit is Rs 250 and the
maximum is Rs 1.5 lakh in a financial year. A deposit must be made every year
or the account becomes irregular; a small penalty is charged to revive it.

## What to watch out for

Partial withdrawal of up to 50 per cent of the balance is allowed for the
girl's higher education after she turns 18 or has passed the tenth standard.
If the account is not operated regularly, the penalty is small but the lost
interest is not, so set a yearly reminder. Remember the 15-year deposit window
is separate from the 21-year maturity, which is the point most people
misunderstand.
