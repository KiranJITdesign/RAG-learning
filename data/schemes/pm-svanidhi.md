# PM Street Vendor's AtmaNirbhar Nidhi (PM SVANidhi)

## What this scheme gives you

A collateral-free working capital loan for urban street vendors, in three
escalating tranches. The first loan is up to Rs 10,000, and on timely
repayment the vendor becomes eligible for a second loan of up to Rs 20,000 and
then a third of up to Rs 50,000.

The loan carries an interest subsidy of 7 per cent per year, credited to the
borrower's account quarterly, and a digital cashback of up to Rs 1,200 a year
for repaying on time and for accepting digital payments. No collateral and no
guarantor are required, which is the whole point of the scheme.

## Who is eligible

- A street vendor who was vending on or before 24 March 2020, with a
  certificate of vending or a letter of recommendation from the Urban Local
  Body.
- Vendors identified in the Urban Local Body survey, and vendors with a
  vending certificate in a vending zone.
- The vendor must be at least 18 years old, must have a bank account, and must
  not have availed of a similar working capital loan from a formal source for
  the same purpose.

## Who is NOT eligible

Vendors who do not have any proof of vending and are not in the Urban Local
Body survey are outside the scheme until they are surveyed and certified.
Vendors who default on an earlier tranche are not eligible for the next one,
because the next tranche depends on repaying the previous one on time.

## Documents you need

Aadhaar, a vending certificate or letter of recommendation from the Urban
Local Body, a bank account linked to Aadhaar, and a mobile number. Vendors who
use an on-loan digital payment app are recorded automatically for the cashback.

## How to apply

Apply on the PM SVANidhi portal or at a bank branch, a Common Service Centre
or an Urban Local Body office. The bank sanctions and disburses the loan,
usually within a month if the documents are complete. The repayment period is
12 months in monthly instalments for the first tranche, so the loan is meant
to be a revolving working capital line rather than a long-term debt.

## What to watch out for

Borrow from a bank or a recognised microfinance lender, not a moneylender
using the scheme's name. Take digital payments and record them, because
transaction volume is what the bank looks at when sanctioning the next, larger
tranche.
