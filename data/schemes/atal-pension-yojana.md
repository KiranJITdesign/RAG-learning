# Atal Pension Yojana

## What this scheme gives you

A guaranteed minimum monthly pension of Rs 1,000, Rs 2,000, Rs 3,000, Rs 4,000
or Rs 5,000 after the subscriber turns 60, depending on the contribution
chosen at the time of joining. The same pension continues to the spouse after
the subscriber's death, and the accumulated corpus is returned to the nominee
if both die.

Contributions are made monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or yearly, and the
amount depends on the entry age and the pension slab chosen. The pension is
guaranteed by the government and is not linked to the stock market.

## Who is eligible

- An Indian citizen in the unorganised sector, aged between 18 and 40 years,
  with a savings bank account and Aadhaar.
- The subscriber must not be an income tax payer. Since October 2022, income
  tax payers are not permitted to join, and the account can be closed if a
  subscriber later starts paying income tax.
- Contributions must continue until the age of 60 to receive the full pension.

## Who is NOT eligible

Income tax payers, and people who already contribute to the Employees'
Provident Fund, the Employees' State Insurance Corporation or the National
Pension System as a government or corporate employee, are not part of the
target group. Those with a bank account but no Aadhaar cannot complete the
KYC.

## Documents you need

A savings bank account, Aadhaar, and a mobile number linked to the account.
Enrolment is done through the bank, so the bank's own account details are
sufficient for the rest of the application.

## How to apply

Approach the bank branch where the savings account is held, or use the bank's
net banking facility to enrol under the Atal Pension Yojana option. The bank
sets up an auto-debit mandate for the contribution. If the contribution is
missed, there is a small penalty and a grace period, and an account funded
below the required amount is treated as irregular.

## What to watch out for

Choose the pension slab keeping the monthly contribution in mind, because
increasing the slab later is not straightforward. If the subscriber exits
before 60, only the accumulated contribution and interest are returned, not
the pension, so this scheme is a long-term commitment rather than a
short-term savings product.
